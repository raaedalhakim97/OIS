# CHAPTER II · EPISODE 5 — "The Elder's Truth"
**Land:** the Long Memory · **Runtime:** ~2:28 · **Form:** the Elder speaks; the world remembers

## LOGLINE
After the drum, the Keeper finally asks the Elder the question he has never asked:
**who are you?** And for the first time, the Elder tells him everything — what a keeper
actually *is*, why the Keeper spent his whole life walking home empty-handed, where the
dark came from, why it wears a keeper's shadow, and whose light the Keeper is really
carrying. Every episode so far turns out to have been hiding this.

## THE REVEALS (each answers something already planted)
| # | The truth | What it explains |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | At the beginning there were **three keepers**, and the world was silent. | why a keeper exists at all |
| 2 | **A keeper's note is given away.** That is the price. | Ep8: *"every night he walked home with empty hands"* |
| 3 | The **first keeper** gave his — then wanted it back, and took it anyway. | the origin of the enemy |
| 4 | Where his note had been became **the first Silence**. His name is **the Sunderer**. | what the Silence *is* — an absence, not a monster |
| 5 | The ones who followed took their notes back too. **The dark wears a keeper's shadow because it was one.** | Ep2/Ep4: why the Hollow is keeper-shaped |
| 6 | The light the Keeper found in the grass was **left there** — it was the **Elder's own note**. | Ep8's discovery; why the Elder always knew where to be |
| 7 | So the Elder now carries **nothing**. | his fading light; sets up his sacrifice |

## FORM
No fight. The Elder talks and **the world remembers** — memory scenes staged on the same
planet in a desaturated, grainy "long memory" grade, cut apart by the Observer's blinks.
The present returns only at the start and the very end.

## WHY THIS EPISODE EARNS ITSELF
It is the hinge of Chapter II: the audience has spent ten episodes watching a small
figure help strangers and walk on alone. This reveals that **the walking on was the
cost**, not the habit — and that the mentor standing beside him has already paid it for
him. It also converts the enemy from a monster into a tragedy: the Sunderer is what a
keeper becomes when he wants his gift back.
