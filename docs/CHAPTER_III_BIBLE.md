# CHAPTER III — "THUS SANG THE KEEPER"
### the cast, the arc, and the rules of the register

Chapter I taught how music works. Chapter II told a story with it. Chapter III is about
**making** it — and it is told in the voice of Nietzsche's *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*: a
man who goes up a mountain with nothing, comes back down with something to say, and says
it in parables to people who did not ask.

**On the source.** *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–85) is long out of copyright, as is
Strauss's tone poem (1896). We are not quoting either. We take the **shape** — the
descent, the parables, the three metamorphoses, the animals, the marketplace — and write
our own words inside it. Anything that sounds like Zarathustra should sound like it
because of its *rhythm*, never because it was copied.

---

## The one idea that makes this a music series

Nietzsche's three metamorphoses of the spirit are, without changing a thing, the three
stages of learning any instrument:

| Metamorphosis | In the book | In music | Chapter III |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Camel** | bears what it is given, kneels for the load | scales, modes, rules, the tradition you must carry | Ep 1–3 |
| **The Lion** | says **No** to the dragon "Thou Shalt" | dissonance, breaking the key, refusing the expected resolution | Ep 4–7 |
| **The Child** | innocence, forgetting, a wheel rolling of itself | improvisation, play, writing your own | Ep 8–10 |

That is the whole chapter, and it is a real pedagogy: learn the rules, break them on
purpose, then make something that needed both.

> *You must carry before you may refuse. You must refuse before you may play.*

## The cast

### The Keeper — *the one who came down*
Twenty episodes of carrying other people's notes, and in Ep10 he gives away the last
thing he owns. He goes up empty. He comes down as a **teacher**, which he is bad at.

**What changed:** he speaks now. In Chapters I–II he was silent and Alan described him.
From Ch III he answers — still in Observian, but in long phrases instead of two-word
ones. His lantern is out for the whole chapter; what light he has, he *makes*.

**His flaw:** he expects to be understood. He is not. That is the marketplace.

### The Eagle — *the proudest note*
A high circling voice: the octave above, and above that. Pride, height, melody, the
line that soars and does not want to come down. Never lands on the tonic if it can help it.

- **Register** +12 to +24 · **Style** confident · **Colour** major
- **Says most often:** *rise* (Re Ti)
- **Wrong when:** height is mistaken for meaning. A melody that never comes home is
  showing off, not singing.

### The Serpent — *the deepest note*
The drone, coiled at the bottom. The tonic that everything returns to. Wisdom, patience,
and **eternal recurrence** made audible: every song comes back to where it started.

- **Register** −12 to −24 · **Style** calm · rarely moves
- **Says most often:** *the same* (Mi Mi) — but where the Silence meant *I copied you*,
  the Serpent means *you will return here*
- **Wrong when:** return becomes refusal to leave. A drone with no melody is a rut.

**The pair is the point:** the Eagle is the melody, the Serpent is the bass. Neither is
music alone. Chapter III is the Keeper learning to be the thing between them — harmony.

### The Rope Dancer — *the one who crosses*
Walks a wire strung between two keys. Chromatic, brave, unsupported by any scale. In the
opening episode of the chapter he **falls**, and the Keeper stays with him while he goes
quiet — the first person in the series the Keeper cannot save.

- Musically: **the tritone**, the interval that belongs to no comfortable place
- His fall is what makes the marketplace laugh, and what makes the Keeper serious

### The Last Man — *the antagonist, and he is not a monster*
The Silence destroyed by copying. The Last Man is worse and quieter: he **flattens**.
He wants music that can never hurt anyone — the same four chords, resolved immediately,
no dissonance, no risk, forever. He is comfortable and he is beloved and the crowd
prefers him.

- Musically: he resolves every tension the instant it appears. He will not allow a
  suspension to hang. Everything is I–V–vi–IV, warm and endless.
- **He cannot be defeated by force**, because he is not attacking. He is offering
  comfort. The only way past him is to make something *worth* the discomfort.
- His line: *why would you want it to hurt?* — and the honest answer takes ten episodes.

### The Marketplace — *the crowd*
Plays only what it already knows. Not evil; busy. They will clap for the Rope Dancer's
fall and hum the Last Man's tune. The Keeper has to learn that he is not speaking to
them — he is speaking to the two or three who stayed behind.

### Alan — *the Observer*
Continues, unchanged in role but changed in relationship: since Ep8 the Keeper can hear
him. In Chapter III they occasionally **disagree**. Alan is the one who watches; the
Keeper is now the one who acts, and the watcher does not always approve.

## The arc, episode by episode

| Ep | Title | Metamorphosis | Teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The One Who Came Down | — | the descent; the Rope Dancer falls; **the tritone** |
| 2 | The Camel's Load | Camel | **modes** — the same seven notes, seven different weights |
| 3 | Thou Shalt | Camel | **key signatures** — the rules written at the start of every line |
| 4 | The Lion's No | Lion | **dissonance** — the sound of refusing to resolve |
| 5 | The Suspension | Lion | holding a note that does not belong, and making them wait |
| 6 | Two Keys and a Wire | Lion | **modulation** — leaving the key on purpose and living |
| 7 | The Last Man's Song | Lion | why I–V–vi–IV is comfortable, and what comfort costs |
| 8 | A Wheel Rolling of Itself | Child | **improvisation** — playing without deciding first |
| 9 | The Same Song Again | Child | **eternal recurrence** as theme and variations |
| 10 | A Dancing Star | Child | he writes one song of his own. Chaos, and what comes of it. |

**Ep 10's title comes from the book's most famous line about chaos giving birth to a
dancing star — and by then the audience will have learned enough theory to write one
themselves.** That is the promise of the whole series, paid off.

## The register — how this must be written

Zarathustra's voice is not archaic decoration. It has rules:

1. **Address someone.** "My brothers." Ours: **"My notes."** Every parable is spoken *to*
   somebody, never into the air.
2. **Declare, do not explain.** *"I teach you the chord that does not resolve."* Then the
   picture explains it. Never both.
3. **Invert the expected proverb.** "You must have chaos in you." Ours: *"A note that has
   never been wrong has never been anywhere."*
4. **Short sentences. Then one long one that runs, and gathers, and arrives.**
5. **"Thus sang the Keeper."** Close the big speeches with the formula. Repetition is the
   point; the audience should be able to say it with him by Ep4.
6. **Never ironic.** This register dies the moment it winks.

**And the hard rule, unchanged since Chapter I:** the theory is never taught as a lesson.
The Camel carries scales because carrying is what a camel does, not because it is
Tuesday's topic.

## Sound design for the chapter

- **The sunrise motif** — C, G, C from the overtone series, established in Ep8 and used
  as the chapter's opening fanfare. It is the chapter's signature; bring it back at Ep10.
- **The Serpent is a permanent drone.** Chapter III has a bottom to it that Chapter II
  did not.
- **The Eagle's line never resolves** until Ep10, when it finally comes down to the tonic
  and the two of them are one chord.
- **The Last Man's music is deliberately pleasant** and should be the catchiest thing in
  the series. If the audience does not enjoy it, the episode fails.

## What carries over

The Observian lexicon, the Observer's Eye blink, the staff, the little planet, the 72 bpm
heartbeat, Alan, the camera, the lighting, matched captions. The Silence is gone. The
Elder is gone — one note never came back, and Chapter III never says which.
